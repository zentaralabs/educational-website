# Backlink plan — Where To Apply

Deliberately minimal. Two tracks, both low-effort, both legitimate. No comment
links, no bought lists, no automated posting, no education "directories" (that
space is almost all link farms — skipped on purpose).

Goal: help Google discover the site faster, earn a handful of real links, and
build brand presence in the places people check when they Google "wheretoapply".

---

## Reusable copy (paste into every submission)

- **Name:** Where To Apply
- **URL:** https://www.wheretoapply.xyz
- **Tagline (short):** Compare Australian universities, courses, fees, and student visas in one place.
- **One-liner (~60 chars):** Australian university & student-visa research for international students
- **Description (medium):**
  > Where To Apply is a free research platform for international students planning to study in Australia. It brings every Australian university's tuition, entry requirements, application deadlines, and scholarships into one structured, filterable database — plus plain-English guides to the student visa (subclass 500), post-study work options, and skilled migration, with a PR points calculator and a live SkillSelect invitation-round tracker. Every figure is sourced from an official university or government page and carries a "last verified" date.
- **Category:** Education / EdTech
- **Founder / maker:** Roman Lama
- **Location:** Nepal
- **Logo:** `public/icon.svg` (export a 512×512 PNG for sites that need raster)
- **Screenshots:** homepage, `/universities` (the filterable directory), `/visas/points-calculator`, `/deadlines`

## Track 2 — Journalist source requests (replaces HARO)

HARO shut down; Connectively (its successor) also shut down in Dec 2024. The live
options:

### Sign up (all free, ~15 min total)

1. **Source of Sources (SOS)** — https://sourceofsources.com — Peter Shankman's
   direct HARO replacement. Enter email → confirm → you get a digest 3×/weekday
   with journalist queries. **Primary.** Choose the "Lifestyle" / "General" and
   "Education" categories if it asks.
2. **Featured** — https://featured.com — sign up as an Expert. Free tier: browse
   open questions, submit written answers; if chosen you're published in the
   article with a byline + link. Fill your expert profile (Roman Lama, founder of
   Where To Apply, focus: studying in Australia / student visas).
3. **Qwoted** — https://qwoted.com — sign up as a Source, free tier. Journalist
   requests matched to your topics.
4. **X / Bluesky** — follow and search **#journorequest** and **#PRrequest**.
   Free, real, and study-abroad / immigration queries come up regularly.

### Weekly workflow (~10 min)

1. Skim the SOS digests + Featured open questions.
2. Anything about: student visas, studying in Australia, international education,
   university costs, choosing a course/country, skilled migration → **forward it
   to me** (or paste the query into a session).
3. I draft the response — specific, quotable, 100–200 words, no fluff, one
   natural mention of Where To Apply and its data.
4. You paste it in and submit under your name.
5. One good placement (a real news / .edu article) beats 50 directory links.

## Guardrails (don't undo the work)

- Never post the link in blog comments, forum threads, or subreddit posts as a
  self-promo drop. Google's link-spam system devalues or penalizes these.
- Never buy an email list or mass-cold-email for links — it flags the sending
  domain as spam and hurts real deliverability.
- Reddit / forums: only ever a genuine, helpful answer to a question someone
  actually asked, posted by you, sparingly. That's outreach, not link-building,
  and it's a separate (later) effort.
- Volume is not the goal. 10 real links over 2 months beats 200 junk links.
